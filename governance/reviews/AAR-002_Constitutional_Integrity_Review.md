# AAR-002 – Constitutional Integrity Review

## 1. Purpose

This Architecture Assurance Review (AAR) validates the constitutional integrity of the foundational governance documents that establish the AI Education Platform's Institutional Knowledge Architecture.

The review confirms that constitutional authority, semantic governance, architectural responsibilities, and document dependencies remain internally coherent, mutually consistent, and free from conflicting definitions.

Successful completion of this review provides assurance that the repository possesses a stable constitutional foundation suitable for establishing the SM-1.0 Constitutional Baseline.

---

# 2. Scope

This review applies to the following constitutional artifacts:

- 000_Architecture_Charter.md
- 002_Product_Constitution.md
- 003_Product_Lexicon.md
- 003a_Semantic_Knowledge_Architecture.md

Downstream architectural artifacts are outside the scope of this review.

---

# 3. Review Objectives

The review verifies that:

- constitutional responsibilities are uniquely assigned
- authority flows in a single direction
- architectural dependencies remain acyclic
- semantic governance is internally consistent
- architectural boundaries are preserved
- terminology remains consistent across all constitutional artifacts
- every governed asset has a single authoritative source

---

# 6. Dependency Validation

The constitutional dependency hierarchy was reviewed.

```
Architecture Charter
        ↓
Product Constitution
        ↓
Product Lexicon
        ↓
Semantic Knowledge Architecture
```

The review confirmed that:

- downstream artifacts inherit constitutional authority
- upstream artifacts remain independent of downstream implementation
- no circular dependencies exist
- authority and dependency remain aligned

---

# 7. Semantic Integrity Validation

The review confirmed that:

- canonical concepts possess a single authoritative definition
- canonical relationships possess a single authoritative definition
- semantic identities remain stable
- canonical representation is governed independently of downstream knowledge models
- knowledge models consume rather than redefine canonical semantics

No semantic inconsistencies requiring architectural redesign were identified.

---

# 8. Boundary Validation

The constitutional boundaries between architectural responsibilities were evaluated.

The review confirmed that:

- principles are not redefined by semantic artifacts
- semantic artifacts do not define behavioral models
- knowledge models do not redefine canonical semantics
- reasoning remains downstream of institutional knowledge
- implementation remains downstream of architectural governance

The separation of constitutional responsibilities has been preserved.

---

# 9. Findings

The review concludes that the constitutional foundation of the repository demonstrates:

- clear authority partitioning
- constitutional dependency integrity
- semantic integrity
- architectural traceability
- governance consistency
- readiness for long-term institutional evolution

No constitutional conflicts were identified.

# 10. Emergent Architectural Principles

During independent architectural reconstruction and peer review, several architectural principles emerged that explain the constitutional structure of the repository, including:

- Authority Partitioning

These principles were treated as interpretive observations during this review and were not used as formal constitutional validation criteria.

Their adoption as explicit constitutional principles, if appropriate, remains subject to the governance process established by the Architecture Charter.
